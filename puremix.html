Case study — 03

Turning a niche library into
a platform people come back to.

Puremix had a loyal community and hundreds of videos. But new users couldn't understand what it was, and the platform was losing ground to competitors. I led the full product redesign as PM.

Company
Puremix
Role
Product Manager
Period
2022 – 2024
Type
B2C SaaS · Edtech · Audio

Context

Founded in 2010, Puremix is a subscription platform for audio engineers — hundreds of tutorial videos on mixing, mastering, recording and production. It had become the reference in its niche, but resting on past success had left it vulnerable.

Puremix wasn't just one product — it was a house of brands:

🎬 Puremix — video streaming
💬 Mixup.audio — collaboration
🔌 Process.audio — plugins

The three products existed in parallel but felt completely disconnected to users. Paying members often didn't know about Mixup or Process. New visitors had no idea what Puremix actually was.

Research

100+ survey responses from current members. This wasn't guesswork — the redesign was built on real behavioral data.

Key insight: The core user behavior is search-first, not browse-first. Most members come with a specific question and search for an answer — not to scroll a feed. This completely changed how we approached navigation and content discovery.

What members wished existed

51.8% — More tips & tricks 50.1% — More advanced content 38.7% — More genre diversity

Why members churned

Cost Not enough time Not enough new content

Strategy

Three business objectives shaped every decision:

  • Make everyone understand what Puremix is — clearly, immediately, on first visit
  • Make the brand resonate beyond the existing community — attract new users
  • Turn the library into a learning + community platform — reduce dependency on expensive new video production

We structured work around three priorities: Clarity (navigation and IA), Content experience (video discovery and free/pro separation), and Community (engagement beyond passive watching).

What we built

Homepage redesign

A clear value proposition above the fold. New visitors now understand within seconds: what Puremix teaches, who the mentors are, what membership gives them. Branding aligned across all three products.

Video library redesign

Cards redesigned with mentor names, categories, and visual identity per genre. Free vs. pro content clearly labelled. Search and filtering overhauled to match the dominant search-first behavior.

Mentor pages

Dedicated profiles for each mentor — background, credits, and video catalog. Mentors were identified in research as the primary reason members trusted and returned to Puremix.

Free vs. pro separation

Explicit content gating with upgrade prompts at natural decision points, without degrading the free experience. Designed to maximize value for both segments while driving conversion.

Branding revamp

Logos and video thumbnails aligned across Puremix, Mixup, and Process. A consistent visual language that signals they're part of the same family — not three different companies.

Takeaways

Clarity is a product feature.

When users can't explain what your product does in one sentence, you have a positioning problem — not a design problem. The redesign started with words before pixels.

Search-first users need a different IA.

The instinct was a beautiful homepage feed. The data said users come with a specific question. That completely changed the navigation and categorization approach.

Community is a retention strategy.

The most sustainable way to reduce churn wasn't more video production — it was giving members reasons to engage with each other at lower cost.

A house of brands needs a unifying story.

Three good products that felt like three different companies. The redesign's biggest unlock wasn't any single feature — it was making the relationship between them visible.
